\chapter{Open form-identification bridges}
\label{chap:open-canonical-bridges}

Closed quadratic structure is only the analytic base.  The following form-level bridges are kept
as named theorems so later work cannot smuggle them into the definition of the selected canonical
constant.  The constant-level bridges used by every route are collected separately in
\cref{chap:shared-analytic-bridges}.

\begin{obstruction}[Raw energy is not a property of an $L^2$ class]
  \label{obs:dirac-raw-energy}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Poincare.Euclidean.dirac_raw_energy,
        FunctionalInequalities.Poincare.Euclidean.dirac_toLp_zero_eq_id}
  \leanok
  Let $\mu=\delta_0$ on $\R$, $f(x)=0$, and $g(x)=x$.  Then $f=g$ in $L^2(\mu)$, but their raw
  gradient energies are respectively $0$ and $1$.  Hence unconditional agreement of raw test
  energies cannot descend to $L^2$ equivalence classes for singular measures.
\end{obstruction}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Both functions take value zero at the support point, while $f'=0$ and $g'=1$ everywhere.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dirichlet property of the smooth relaxation]
  \label{thm:smooth-gradient-relaxation-dirichlet}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Energy.NormalContraction.smoothed,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.NormalContraction.contDiff_smoothed,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.NormalContraction.lipschitzWith_smoothed,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.NormalContraction.norm_smoothed_sub_le,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.compSmoothed,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.energy_compSmoothed_le,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.norm_toL2_compSmoothed_sub_le,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.relaxedContractionBound,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.markovian,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.dirichlet,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Intrinsic.markovian,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Intrinsic.dirichlet,
        Solutions.ThmSmoothGradientRelaxationDirichlet.certificate}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:smooth-gradient-relaxation-closed}
  For every finite-dimensional Borel probability $\mu$, the ambient smooth-gradient relaxation
  is Markovian: every normal contraction of an $L^2(\mu)$ class decreases its relaxed energy.
  Together with closed quadratic structure, it therefore defines a Dirichlet energy.
  In Lean a normal contraction is \texttt{Energy.NormalContraction} ($1$-Lipschitz, fixing $0$)
  acting on $L^2(\mu)$ classes through Mathlib's \texttt{LipschitzWith.compLp}, and the
  conclusion is \texttt{Energy.Dirichlet}~$\mu$~\texttt{(SmoothGradient.relaxation}~$\mu$\texttt{)}.
  The certificate also records the same Dirichlet property for the intrinsic-affine relaxation
  \texttt{SmoothGradient.Intrinsic.energy}~$\mu$ on the ambient $L^2(\mu)$.  No absolute
  continuity or density is assumed: the law may be singular, and dimension zero is included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  Mollify the contraction on the real line.  The mollified contraction $\varphi_j$ is smooth,
  $1$-Lipschitz and uniformly within $2/(j+1)$ of $\varphi$.  For a smooth test $f$ the
  composition $\varphi_j\circ f$ is again a smooth test with $\norm{\nabla(\varphi_j\circ f)}\le
  \norm{\nabla f}$ pointwise, hence with energy at most that of $f$, and its $L^2$ class converges
  to $\varphi\circ f$.  This is the relaxed contraction bound on every realized test, and the
  reusable contraction-transfer theorem extends it to every $L^2$ class by lower semicontinuity.
  The contraction is mollified rather than the test, so no Rademacher, density or absolute
  continuity input is needed.  The intrinsic statement follows because passing to intrinsic
  coordinates commutes with the contraction almost everywhere.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Identification with Cheeger energy]
  \label{thm:smooth-gradient-relaxation-two-cheeger}
  \uses{def:selected-canonical-frontend}
  On the affine support, let $\Ch_\mu$ denote the standard metric Cheeger energy normalized as
  one half of the lower-semicontinuous relaxation of the squared local slope.  After transporting
  it to ambient real $L^2(\mu)$, prove
  \[
    \mathcal E^{\mathrm{can}}_\mu=2\Ch_\mu.
  \]
\end{theorem}

The factor $2$ is part of the intended normalization, not an established declaration. The
statement choice this node deferred is now made, so \texttt{notready} is removed. The Cheeger
energy to formalize is the local-slope convention: one half of the sequential
lower-semicontinuous relaxation of the squared local slope over the literature test class
(\texttt{Energy.Cheeger.energy}), transported to the affine support by the construction of
\cref{def:intrinsic-affine-energy} (\texttt{Energy.Cheeger.Intrinsic.energy}). The
asymptotic-Lipschitz relaxation is defined separately, and its agreement with the local-slope
relaxation is carried as its own explicit obligation instead of being imposed by definition. What
remains is an open proof rather than an open statement, and the absence of \texttt{leanok}
already records that: for singular measures neither the comparison of the two relaxations nor the
identification of the smooth-gradient relaxation with the relaxed squared local slope has a source
recorded in the bibliography. Those two obligations, and the three statements that follow from
them, are the whole content of \path{Energy/WIP/Cheeger.lean}, which is not imported by the
aggregate and has no certificate. The absolutely continuous scope is separated out as
\cref{cor:absolutely-continuous-relaxation-two-cheeger} and is certified.

\begin{corollary}[Cheeger identification for absolutely continuous laws]
  \label{cor:absolutely-continuous-relaxation-two-cheeger}
  \uses{def:selected-canonical-frontend,lem:logconcave-intrinsic-absolute-continuity}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Energy.Cheeger.energy,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.Cheeger.Intrinsic.energy,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.relaxation_eq_localSlope_of_absolutelyContinuous,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Intrinsic.energy_eq_two_mul_cheeger_of_absolutelyContinuous,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.LogConcave.intrinsicSmooth_energy_eq_two_mul_cheeger,
        Solutions.CorAbsolutelyContinuousRelaxationTwoCheeger.certificate}
  \leanok
  Let $\mu$ be a Borel probability law whose intrinsic law on the direction space of its affine
  support is absolutely continuous with respect to some additive Haar measure there.  Then
  \[
    \mathcal E^{\mathrm{can}}_\mu=2\Ch_\mu
  \]
  on ambient real $L^2(\mu)$.  In particular this holds for every compact-set log-concave law,
  whose intrinsic law is absolutely continuous by
  \cref{lem:logconcave-intrinsic-absolute-continuity}, so the identification is available
  throughout the KLS routes without the singular case.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  One inequality is unconditional: a globally smooth test is an admissible local-slope test with
  the same energy, so relaxing the squared local slope over the larger class gives the smaller
  functional, $\Ch$-side $\le$ canonical side.  For the reverse inequality, absolute continuity
  supplies the certified weighted smooth approximation: every locally Lipschitz $L^2$ test of
  finite slope energy has a sequence of globally smooth tests converging to it in $L^2$ with
  gradient energies converging to its slope energy, which is a recovery sequence for the
  smooth-gradient pre-energy.  Hence the two relaxations agree, and halving gives
  $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{can}}=2\Ch$ after transport along the intrinsic $L^2$ isometry.  The
  log-concave case is this statement applied to the canonical Haar measure of the direction
  space.  Nothing here touches the singular comparisons of
  \cref{thm:smooth-gradient-relaxation-two-cheeger}.
\end{proof}

\begin{obstruction}[Ambient raw energy sees normal derivatives]
  \label{obs:normal-direction-raw-energy}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.normalCoordinate,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.normalCoordinate_ae_eq_zero,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.gradient_normalCoordinate,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.smoothTest_energy_normalCoordinate,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.normalCoordinate,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.toL2_normalCoordinate,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.exists_test_toL2_eq_zero_energy_eq_one,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.not_respectsAmbientFibers}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:affine-support-intrinsic-law,def:smooth-gradient-preenergy}
  Let the affine support of $\mu$ be a proper affine subspace $A=a+V$.  If $n\in V^\perp$ is a
  unit vector and $u(x)=\inner{n}{x-a}$, then $u=0$ in $L^2(\mu)$ whereas
  \[
    \int\norm{\nabla u}^2\dd\mu=1.
  \]
  Thus ambient raw energy depends on the extension away from $A$, even when the intrinsic law has
  a regular density.  In Lean the properness of $A$ is the hypothesis that the direction space
  of the affine support is not the whole space, $a$ is the chosen support point, and the
  conclusion is recorded both as the pair of values $0$ and $1$ for the smooth test $u$ and as
  the failure of \texttt{RespectsAmbientFibers} for the ambient smooth-gradient pre-energy.
\end{obstruction}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The function vanishes on $A$, which carries $\mu$, while its ambient gradient is the constant
  unit vector $n$; a unit normal exists because the orthogonal complement of a proper subspace
  of a finite-dimensional inner-product space is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{question}[Sequential cores and graph-norm cores]
  \label{q:smooth-gradient-raw-core-agreement}
  \uses{obs:dirac-raw-energy,obs:normal-direction-raw-energy,
        def:smooth-gradient-preenergy,def:smooth-gradient-relaxation}
  Beyond the density hypotheses below, determine when raw smooth-gradient energy descends through
  the $L^2$ realization and satisfies the project predicate \texttt{IsSequentialCoreFor}.  On a
  proper affine support the statement must be intrinsic, or must quotient out normal derivatives.
  Separately, construct the finite-energy domain with its graph norm and prove the precise
  implication or equivalence between graph-norm density and \texttt{IsSequentialCoreFor} under
  closed-quadratic hypotheses.  No such equivalence is assumed definitionally.
\end{question}

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic raw/sequential-core agreement under density hypotheses]
  \label{thm:smooth-gradient-raw-core-agreement-density}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Probability.LocallyDominatesOn,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.WeightedGradient.operator_isClosable_of_isOpen,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.LocalDensity,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.LocalDensity.absolutelyContinuous,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.LocalDensity.frontier_support_null,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.LocalDensity.locallyDominatesOn,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.lipschitzOperator_isClosable,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.exists_domain_sequence_tendsto,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.toL2_mem_closure_domain,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.lipschitzRelaxation_le_relaxation,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.preEnergy_agreesOnTests,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.preEnergy_isSequentialCoreFor,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Intrinsic.preEnergy_agreesOnTests,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Intrinsic.preEnergy_isSequentialCoreFor,
        Solutions.ThmSmoothGradientRawCoreAgreementDensity.certificate}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:affine-support-intrinsic-law,def:smooth-gradient-preenergy,
        def:smooth-gradient-relaxation}
  Let $A=a+V$ be the affine support of $\mu$, and write $\nu$ for its intrinsic law.  Assume that
  there are an open set $\Omega\subseteq V$ and a Borel function $\rho:V\to[0,\infty)$ such that
  $\nu(V\setminus\Omega)=0$ and, for every Borel set $B\subseteq\Omega$,
  \[
    \nu(B)=\int_B\rho\dd\mathrm{Leb}_V.
  \]
  Assume also that $\rho>0$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$ and that
  $\rho,\rho^{-1}\in L^\infty_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mathrm{Leb}_V)$. The explicit positivity
  premise is retained: real inversion in Lean sends zero to zero, so bounded reciprocal alone
  does not encode positivity. Write $P_\nu$ for the
  intrinsic smooth-gradient pre-energy and
  \[
    \mathcal E_\nu=P_\nu.\mathrm{sequentialRelaxation}.
  \]
  Then $P_\nu.\texttt{AgreesOnTests}(\mathcal E_\nu)$.  Consequently
  $P_\nu.\texttt{IsSequentialCoreFor}(\mathcal E_\nu)$, since
  \texttt{SequentiallyGenerates} is definitional for $\mathcal E_\nu$.
  In Lean the density hypotheses are the structure
  \texttt{Energy.SmoothGradient.LocalDensity}~$\nu$~$m$ for a Haar measure $m$ on the direction
  space: the open set $\Omega$, $\nu(\Omega^c)=0$, the density representation of $\nu$ on
  Borel subsets of $\Omega$, positivity of $\rho$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$, and local
  essential boundedness of $\rho^{-1}$ on compact subsets of $\Omega$.  The local
  boundedness of $\rho$ itself is not needed for the proof and is not assumed.  The theorem is
  proved for every probability law on a finite-dimensional inner-product space with a local
  density; the intrinsic law is the named instance.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The density hypotheses give $\nu\ll m$, $\Omega\subseteq\operatorname{int}\operatorname{supp}\nu$
  (so the frontier of the support is $\nu$-null), and local domination of $m$ by $\nu$ on the
  compact subsets of $\Omega$.  The unit-weight gradient operator $T$ on globally Lipschitz
  $L^2(\nu)$ tests is therefore closable: the closability argument of
  \cref{lem:covariance-core-closure} is carried out on an arbitrary open carrier $\Omega$ with
  local domination instead of the interior of the support.  For a smooth test $f$ the cutoffs
  $\chi_R f$ are globally Lipschitz compactly supported tests converging to $f$ in graph norm,
  so $f\in\operatorname{dom}\bar T$ with $\bar Tf=\nabla f$ and
  $\|\bar Tf\|^2=\int\norm{\nabla f}^2\,d\nu$.  The same cutoffs are recovery sequences,
  so the relaxation of $\|Tg\|^2$ is at most $\mathcal E_\nu$; the relaxation of a closable
  operator is $\|\bar T\cdot\|^2$ on the domain of the closure.  Hence
  $\int\norm{\nabla f}^2\,d\nu=\|\bar Tf\|^2\le\mathcal E_\nu([f])\le P_\nu(f)$, which is
  agreement on tests; sequential generation is definitional.
\end{proof}

This gives a density-case answer to \cref{q:smooth-gradient-raw-core-agreement}; the question is
not a logical premise of the theorem.  The three typewriter names above refer to the existing Lean
predicates in \path{FunctionalInequalities/Energy/FormCore.lean}; graph-norm density is deliberately
left in the question rather than conflated with that interface.  By
\cref{thm:borell-logconcave-density}, the displayed density hypotheses hold for every
finite-dimensional log-concave law in intrinsic coordinates.

The two obstructions isolate representative dependence from the remaining density question.
Neither the Dirichlet property nor a Cheeger identification is declared as a dependency until an
actual proof uses it.  Intrinsic raw/core agreement is an optional, strictly stronger testwise
identification; it is not a premise of the smooth-test--relaxed constant bridge.

\begin{lemma}[Smooth tests restrict and extend along affine support]
  \label{lem:smooth-tests-affine-restriction-extension}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.exists_intrinsic_restriction,
        FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.Test.exists_ambient_extension}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:smooth-gradient-preenergy,def:affine-support-intrinsic-law,
        thm:intrinsic-l2-equivalence}
  Let $\mu$ be a finite-dimensional Borel probability, with affine support $a+V$ and intrinsic
  law $\nu$. Restriction gives an intrinsic smooth finite-energy test $g(v)=f(a+v)$ with the
  transported $L^2$ realization and no greater raw energy. Every intrinsic test extends by
  $f(x)=g(\operatorname{proj}_V(x-a))$, with the corresponding realization and the same raw energy.
  Zero-dimensional support is included.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
  The chain rule bounds gradient norms under smooth contractions. Reconstruction and orthogonal
  projection preserve the respective laws. Restricting the constructed extension gives the
  original test, so the two energy inequalities force equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ambient and intrinsic relaxations agree]
  \label{thm:ambient-intrinsic-relaxation-equality}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Energy.SmoothGradient.relaxation_eq_intrinsic_energy}
  \leanok
  \uses{def:smooth-gradient-relaxation,def:intrinsic-affine-energy,thm:intrinsic-l2-equivalence,
        lem:smooth-tests-affine-restriction-extension}
  Let $\mu$ be a Borel probability law on a finite-dimensional real Hilbert space.  Transport the
  smooth-gradient relaxation of its intrinsic law back along the intrinsic $L^2$ equivalence, as
  in \cref{def:intrinsic-affine-energy}.  Then, on the common carrier $L^2(\mu)$,
  \[
    \mathcal E^{\mathrm{amb}}_\mu(f)
      =\mathcal E^{\mathrm{intr}}_\mu(f)
    \qquad\text{for every }f\in L^2(\mu).
  \]
  This includes laws on proper affine subspaces and the zero-dimensional case.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
   Restricting an ambient smooth test to the affine support projects its gradient onto the
  tangent space, so it cannot increase raw energy and gives one inequality after relaxation.
  Conversely, composing an intrinsic smooth test with the orthogonal projection onto the affine
  support gives a globally smooth ambient extension, constant in every normal direction, with
  exactly the same raw energy.  Applying both operations termwise to approximating sequences gives
  the reverse inequality.  In dimension zero the constant extension has zero energy on both sides.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ambient and intrinsic-affine constants agree]
  \label{cor:ambient-intrinsic-constant-equality}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.Poincare.SmoothGradient.Ambient.constant_eq_intrinsic,
        FunctionalInequalities.Poincare.SmoothGradient.Ambient.holds_iff_intrinsic}
  \leanok
  \uses{thm:ambient-intrinsic-relaxation-equality,def:l2-energy-poincare-constant,
        def:selected-canonical-frontend}
  For every finite-dimensional Borel probability law $\mu$,
  \[
    \CE\bigl(\mu,\mathcal E^{\mathrm{amb}}_\mu\bigr)=\CPCan(\mu)
  \]
  as extended nonnegative constants, including the value $+\infty$. The corresponding
  finite-constant predicates agree for every $C\ge0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \leanok
   The two energies are equal on the same $L^2(\mu)$ carrier by the preceding theorem, so
  their finite-energy domains, Rayleigh quotients, and extended suprema coincide.
\end{proof}

Although intrinsic coordinates use an affine origin, changing that origin only translates the
intrinsic law.  Translation conjugates the two $L^2$ carriers isometrically and preserves
variance and gradient energy.  The energy equality and the constant equality are therefore
independent of the chosen affine origin.

The preferred implementation order is the exact ambient/intrinsic equality, the direct
smooth-test--relaxed bridge of \cref{chap:shared-analytic-bridges}, and then the literature test-density
bridge.  The first two are now proved in Lean, with two local certificates and unchanged reviewed signatures.
Dirichlet and Cheeger identifications follow as form enhancements.  A raw/core theorem
with explicit singular-measure hypotheses remains useful, but it is not on this main path.
