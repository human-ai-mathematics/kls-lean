\chapter{Four routes to KLS}
\label{chap:kls-routes}

This chapter is a roadmap imported from the pinned upstream commit \sourcecommit.  Its nodes have
different upstream statuses: open gates, conditional implications, and proof dossiers marked
\texttt{proved}/\texttt{checked\_by: agent}.  Those statuses are reported route by route.  None of
these upstream statements is Lean-certified.  The only \texttt{leanok} in this chapter is the
implemented definition \cref{def:conditional-fiber-form}, whose well-definedness contracts are proved;
no theorem, corollary, assumption or question here carries \texttt{leanok}.

The TeX command \texttt{\textbackslash uses} means logical AND.  The four sufficient routes,
however, are alternatives.  Their logical composition is therefore recorded in
\texttt{route-composition.yaml}: the target is an \texttt{any\_of}, while the named nodes inside
each route form an \texttt{all\_of}.  In particular, \cref{conj:kls} intentionally has no
\texttt{uses} edge to all four routes.  This logical OR asserts neither mathematical independence
nor probabilistic independence; the routes share the bridges of
\cref{chap:shared-analytic-bridges}.

The target's \texttt{adapters} entry in the route sidecar is repository-facing alignment
metadata, not a list of logical prerequisites for KLS.  In particular,
\cref{thm:selected-literature-constant-equivalence} is not a \texttt{uses} edge of the route
consumers below.  Logical uses remain explicit at the consuming node; hence the existing
\cref{thm:affine-isotropic-reduction} edges are retained where that reduction is used.

\section{Eldan localization}
\label{route:eldan-localization}

The route follows the evolution of a balanced fixed cut under stochastic localization, with a
two-color covariance/excess decomposition.  The live gate is weaker than the older all-cut
Carleson package.  Eldan introduced the underlying scheme \cite{Eldan2013ThinShell}; Lee and
Vempala obtained quantitative isoperimetric, concentration, and mixing bounds toward KLS
\cite{LeeVempala2024}.  Neither paper proves the tight-prefix gate below or the full KLS
conjecture.

\begin{assumption}[Tight-prefix absorptive Carleson estimate]
  \label{ass:tight-prefix-carleson}
  There are universal $\eta\in(0,1/6]$, $T_0,C_0,C_1>0$, and $\alpha<1$ such that, for every
  isotropic log-concave $\mu$, every fixed cut $E$ with
  $\abs{\mu(E)-1/2}\le\eta/2$, and every $0<T\le T_0$,
  \[
    \E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_\eta}S_t\dd t
    \le C_0T+C_1\E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_\eta}r_t\dd t
       +\alpha\E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_\eta}D_t\dd t.
  \]
  Here $S_t,r_t,D_t$ and the tight stopping time $\tau_\eta$ are the two-color quantities of the
  pinned localization manuscript.
\end{assumption}

\begin{corollary}[Tight-window consumption]
  \label{cor:tight-window-consumption}
  \uses{ass:tight-prefix-carleson,thm:logconcave-cheeger-poincare-comparison}
  The tight-prefix estimate controls the stopped scalar Riccati evolution and yields the
  dimension-free boundary lower bound for every balanced near-minimizing cut.  The standard
  balanced near-minimizer reduction then yields KLS when the assumption holds universally.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Integrate the stopped Riccati identity, absorb the $D_t$ term using $\alpha<1$, and apply
  Gronwall.  The resulting uniform stopped bound feeds the localization survival estimate.  This
  proof is upstream agent-reviewed, not Lean-certified.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Historical refuted weighted package}
\label{route:eldan-weighted-package}

The following package is retained only to make a failed historical branch auditable.  It is not a
fifth route alternative and is not an input to the live tight-prefix package.

\begin{assumption}[Refuted literal weighted near-Cheeger package]
  \label{ass:weighted-package}
  There are universal constants $T_0,C_0,C_1,C_2,\gamma>0$,
  $\eta\in(0,1/4]$, and $\beta\in[0,1/2)$ with $2\beta+64\eta^2<1$ such that every balanced
  finite-perimeter cut with initial excess $e_0\le1$ satisfies both a superlinear weighted-excess
  estimate
  \[
    \E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_\eta}
      e_t\bigl(1+\norm{A_t}_\op\bigr)^{5/2}\dd t
      \le C_2\bigl(Te_0+T^{1+\gamma}\bigr)
  \]
  and the paired absorptive weighted Stein-trace estimate, uniformly for $0<T\le T_0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Exponential-spectator obstruction]
  \label{prop:weighted-spectator-obstruction}
  For every proposed $C,T_0,\gamma>0$, $\eta\in(0,1/2)$, and $\delta>0$, some balanced cylinder
  in an isotropic product of centered one-sided exponentials has additive and relative initial
  excess at most $\delta$ but violates the displayed weighted-excess estimate at a time
  $0<T\le T_0$.  Thus \cref{ass:weighted-package} is false with its stated global quantifiers.
  The product witnesses do not refute KLS.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The pinned upstream proof uses an independent exponential spectator to make the global
  covariance weight large while preserving the cut geometry.  It is agent-reviewed, not
  Lean-certified.
\end{proof}

\section{Moment-map spectral route}
\label{route:moment-map-spectral}

This route tracks a normalized first eigenfunction through localization, retaining the directional
posterior covariance information that a scalar covariance bound discards.

\begin{question}[Fixed-eigenfunction full-damping occupation]
  \label{q:mm-spectral-occupation}
  On smooth strongly log-concave isotropic approximants, do universal $T_0,C_0,C_1>0$ exist such
  that the normalized first eigenfunction quantities $g_t,H_t,A_t$ satisfy, for $0<t\le T_0$,
  \[
    \E\int_0^t\norm{H_s}_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\dd s
    \le C_0t+C_1\E\int_0^t\abs{g_s}^2\dd s
       +\E\int_0^t2g_s^\mathsf{T}A_sg_s\dd s,
  \]
  uniformly through approximation?
\end{question}

\begin{proposition}[Full-damping sufficiency]
  \label{prop:spectral-sufficiency}
  \uses{q:mm-spectral-occupation,thm:affine-isotropic-reduction}
  If the occupation estimate holds, set
  \[
    M_*=(1+C_0T_0)e^{C_1T_0},
    \qquad
    T_* = \min\{T_0,(2M_*)^{-1}\}.
  \]
  Then every isotropic log-concave law satisfies $\CPLit\le2/T_*$.  Hence the estimate implies
  KLS; no strict damping surplus or convergence of eigenfunctions is needed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  It\^o's formula for $\E\abs{g_t}^2$ contains the Hessian occupation and the full damping term
  with opposite signs.  The hypothesis cancels the latter, Gronwall bounds the remaining moment,
  and posterior variance conservation gives the spectral-gap estimate.  This is an upstream
  conditional argument only.
\end{proof}

\section{Moment-map CMH route}
\label{route:moment-map-cmh}

The CMH route is a sufficient-condition program, not a reformulation of KLS.  It asks only for one
well-chosen regular recovery sequence with bounded canonical moment-Hessian quotient.  The regular
moment-map background is classical work of Berman--Berndtsson and Fathi
\cite{BermanBerndtsson2013RealMA,Fathi2019SteinMomentMaps}; the recovery-envelope gate below is a
separate repository statement.

\begin{theorem}[CMH recovery controls the affine Poincar\'e constant]
  \label{thm:cmh-recovery-affine-poincare-bound}
  \uses{def:log-concave-law,def:affine-poincare-constant,
        thm:affine-covariance-relaxation-closed}
  Let $\mu$ be a centered log-concave probability law on a fixed finite-dimensional Euclidean
  space, and let $(\mu_k)$ be a centered, full-dimensional, compactly supported log-concave
  sequence in the pinned source's regular compact-target moment-map class.  If
  $W_2(\mu_k,\mu)\to0$ in the original ambient coordinates, then
  \[
    C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu)
      \le \liminf_{k\to\infty}C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu_k)
      \le \liminf_{k\to\infty}\CMH(\mu_k).
  \]
  Here regularity includes the source's canonical moment-map kernel and weak zero-flux closed
  Stein-generator convention.  The limiting law may have proper affine support.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  This packages two pinned upstream results.  The first inequality is the record
  \[
    \texttt{lem:affine-poincare-w2-liminf},
  \]
  and the second follows pointwise from
  \[
    \texttt{thm:cmh-implies-affine-poincare}.
  \]
  The closed covariance form in those results is identified with the present relaxation by
  \cref{thm:affine-covariance-relaxation-closed}.  These upstream results are agent-reviewed, not
  Lean-certified here.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded CMH recovery envelope]
  \label{ass:cmh-recovery-envelope}
  There is a universal $C$ such that, for every finite-dimensional Euclidean space $E$ and every
  centered log-concave probability law $\mu$ on $E$, there is a centered, full-dimensional,
  compactly supported log-concave sequence $(\mu_k)$ on the same space $E$, belonging to the
  pinned source's regular compact-target moment-map class, such that
  \[
    W_2(\mu_k,\mu)\longrightarrow0,
    \qquad
    \liminf_{k\to\infty}\CMH(\mu_k)\le C.
  \]
\end{assumption}

\begin{corollary}[One CMH recovery sequence suffices]
  \label{cor:cmh-recovery-sequence-suffices}
  \uses{ass:cmh-recovery-envelope,def:affine-poincare-constant,
        thm:cmh-recovery-affine-poincare-bound,
        lem:affine-poincare-translation-invariance,thm:affine-isotropic-reduction}
  Under the bounded recovery-envelope assumption, every centered finite-dimensional log-concave
  law satisfies
  \[
    C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu)\le C.
  \]
  Both log-concavity and $C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}$ are invariant under translations, so the
  same bound holds for every finite-dimensional log-concave law.  By item~(3) of
  \cref{thm:affine-isotropic-reduction}, the assumption therefore implies KLS, including
  degeneration to a proper affine support.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  Apply \cref{thm:cmh-recovery-affine-poincare-bound} to the sequence supplied by
  \cref{ass:cmh-recovery-envelope}.  For an arbitrary law, translate by its negative barycenter
  and use \cref{lem:affine-poincare-translation-invariance} before applying item~(3) of
  \cref{thm:affine-isotropic-reduction}.
\end{proof}

\section{Conditional-fiber frame route}
\label{route:conditional-fiber-frame}

The frame may depend on the measure, but it must be chosen before the test function.  One finite
constant must work for all positive dimensions and all eligible laws.  The route is existential
over all admissible frames; failure of the natural simplex root frame does not refute it.
The frame construction below is restricted to $d\ge1$: in dimension zero the unit sphere is empty
and carries no probability frame.  The zero-dimensional KLS conclusion is handled separately by
zero variance.

\begin{definition}[Conditional-fiber form]
  \label{def:conditional-fiber-form}
  \lean{FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.Admissible,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.jointLaw,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.baseLaw,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.kernel,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.fiberEnergy,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.energy,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.form,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.domain,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.lintegral_baseLaw,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.ae_memLp_id,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.measurable_fiberEnergy,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.energyWith_eq,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.energy_congr_ae,
        FunctionalInequalities.KLS.ConditionalFiber.form_toLp}
  \leanok
  Let $d\ge1$.  For a Borel probability law $\mu$ on $\R^d$ with finite second moment, let $\rho$ be an
  even probability law on the sphere with
  \[
    d\int_{S^{d-1}}\theta\theta^{\mathsf T}\dd\rho(\theta)=I_d.
  \]
  Under $\rho\otimes\mu$, disintegrate $T=\inner{x}{\theta}$ over
  $(\theta,z)=(\theta,P_{\theta^\perp}x)$.  This gives a jointly measurable conditional kernel
  $\mu_{\theta,z}$; write $\bar\mu_\theta=(P_{\theta^\perp})_\#\mu$.  Define
  \[
    \mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)
    =d\int_{S^{d-1}}\int_{\theta^\perp}
      \frac{\Var_{\mu_{\theta,z}}(f(z+T\theta))}
           {\Var_{\mu_{\theta,z}}(T)}
      \dd\bar\mu_\theta(z)\dd\rho(\theta),
  \]
  with exceptional fibers, zero-variance fibers, and fibers with infinite conditional second
  moment contributing zero.
  Finite second moment makes the last case null for the joint base law
  $\dd\rho(\theta)\dd\bar\mu_\theta(z)$.  The value is independent of conditional versions and
  of the Borel representative of $f\in L^2(\mu)$.  The nonnegative integrals take values
  in $[0,\infty]$; the maximal form domain consists of the $L^2(\mu)$ classes with finite
  $\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}$.
  The Lean integral construction is implemented, and its six base-law and well-definedness
  contracts are proved: the fixed product base integrates as the iterated integral over
  directions and projected laws; finite second moment makes infinite conditional second
  moments a null set of base points; the guarded normalized variance is jointly measurable
  for every Borel test; every Markov version of the same disintegration gives the same value;
  $\mu$-a.e.\ equal Borel tests give the same value; and the chosen $L^2$ representative agrees
  with the Borel formula.  This is a definition node: no closedness, Markov, frame-bound or
  Poincar\'e statement is asserted here.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Closed conditional-fiber form]
  \label{lem:conditional-fiber-form}
  \uses{def:conditional-fiber-form}
  For $d\ge1$ and a full-dimensional log-concave probability with density and finite
  second moment, the form
  is densely defined, closed, symmetric, Markovian, and reversible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  This is the structural part of the pinned upstream node. On a fiber of variance
  $0<\sigma^2<\infty$, the normalized variance is
  $(2\sigma^2)^{-1}\iint(f(t)-f(s))^2\,d\mu_{\theta,z}(t)d\mu_{\theta,z}(s)$.
  Disintegrating and integrating gives a nonnegative symmetric jump form. An $L^2$-convergent
  sequence has an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence; disintegration and Fatou make
  this form lower semicontinuous, hence closed on its maximal domain. Lipschitz scalar
  contractions decrease every squared difference, proving the Markov property.
  Symmetry of the two-point measure gives reversibility.

  For density, let $f$ be globally $L$-Lipschitz. Finite second moment gives $f\in L^2(\mu)$.
  On a fiber with $0<\Var(T)<\infty$, let $T'$ be an independent copy of $T$. Since
  $\norm{\theta}=1$,
  \[
    \Var(f(z+T\theta))
      =\tfrac12\mathbb E\bigl[|f(z+T\theta)-f(z+T'\theta)|^2\bigr]
      \le L^2\Var(T).
  \]
  Dividing by the positive variance and integrating over the probability base gives
  $\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)\le dL^2$; the other fibers contribute zero by definition.
  In particular, $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^d)$ lies in the form domain and is dense in $L^2(\mu)$.
  This density argument needs neither log-concavity nor a one-dimensional Poincar\'e bound.
  The stated scope is retained to match the imported structural result. The comparison with
  integrated gradient energy below is a separate use of the numerical input.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional-fiber comparison with an explicit input constant]
  \label{lem:conditional-fiber-energy-comparison}
  \uses{def:conditional-fiber-form,def:literature-poincare-constant,
        lem:finite-energy-factor-poincare}
  Let $d\ge1$, let $\mu\ll\mathrm{Leb}_{\mathbb R^d}$ have finite second moment, and let
  $\rho$ be an admissible frame. Suppose one finite $\kappa\ge0$ satisfies
  $C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{lit}}(\mu_{\theta,z})\le
  \kappa\Var_{\mu_{\theta,z}}(T)$ for almost every nondegenerate conditional fiber.
  Every locally Lipschitz $f\in L^2(\mu)$ with finite gradient energy then satisfies
  \[
    \mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)\le\kappa\int\norm{\nabla f}^2\dd\mu<\infty.
  \]
  Hence $\Var_\mu(f)\le C\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)$ on the maximal finite form domain,
  for finite $C\ge0$, implies $C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{lit}}(\mu)\le\kappa C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  A finite upper bound for the literature infimum is itself valid: choose admissible constants
  below that bound plus $1/j$ and pass to the limit for each fixed finite-energy test.
  Disintegration gives square integrability of the restricted test and integrability of its
  directional derivative on almost every fiber. Rademacher's theorem, absolute continuity,
  and Fubini identify that derivative with $\langle\nabla f,\theta\rangle$. Dividing the
  assumed fiber inequality by its positive variance and using Tonelli gives
  \begin{align*}
    \mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)
      &\le \kappa d\int_{S^{d-1}}\int
                |\langle\nabla f(x),\theta\rangle|^2\,d\mu(x)d\rho(\theta)\\
      &=\kappa\int|\nabla f(x)|^2\,d\mu(x).
  \end{align*}
  The last equality is the frame identity. Conditional Dirac laws have zero numerator;
  exceptional or infinite-second-moment fibers are null. The resulting finite bound puts the
  test in the maximal form domain before the form-gap inequality is applied. The final
  multiplication by $C$ is the abstract implication in \cref{lem:finite-energy-factor-poincare}.
  The actual disintegration and directional-energy calculation here is an analytic proof,
  not an application already elaborated in Lean.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Verified log-concave fiber specialization]
  \label{cor:conditional-fiber-twelve-bound}
  \uses{lem:conditional-fiber-energy-comparison,thm:one-dimensional-logconcave-poincare}
  For a full-dimensional log-concave law with finite second moment,
  $\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)\le12\int|\nabla f|^2\,d\mu$ on the literature test domain.
  A finite form-gap bound $C$ therefore gives $\CPLit(\mu)\le12C$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  For each fixed direction, Fubini disintegration of the log-concave density gives conditional
  densities proportional to $t\mapsto\rho_\mu(z+t\theta)$ wherever the normalizing slice
  integral is finite and positive. These densities are log-concave. The remaining fibers are
  null; finite second moment gives finite conditional variances almost everywhere. Apply
  \cref{thm:one-dimensional-logconcave-poincare} and then the preceding comparison with
  $\kappa=12$. The same argument accepts any independently established smaller universal input.
\end{proof}

\begin{question}[Test-independent conditional-fiber frame]
  \label{q:conditional-fiber-frame}
  \uses{def:conditional-fiber-form}
  Is there a finite $C\ge0$ such that, for every integer $d\ge1$ and every full-dimensional
  isotropic log-concave $\mu$ on $\R^d$, there is an admissible frame $\rho_\mu$, fixed
  independently of $f$, satisfying
  \[
    \Var_\mu(f)\le C\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho_\mu}(f)
  \]
  for every $f$ in the maximal closed form domain?
\end{question}

An affirmative answer together with \cref{lem:conditional-fiber-form,cor:conditional-fiber-twelve-bound}
gives the isotropic KLS bound $\CPLit\le12C$ in every positive dimension; an input $\kappa$
gives $\kappa C$. In dimension zero the Poincar\'e constant is
zero.  Applying \cref{thm:affine-isotropic-reduction} then gives the full KLS statement, including
laws on proper affine supports.  There is no
upstream theorem labelled \texttt{thm:conditional-fiber-frame-suffices}; the route sidecar uses
the structural upstream node and frame question together with the local parameterized comparison,
verified numerical input and affine/isotropic bridge. It does not attribute the new comparison
statement to the upstream ledger.
