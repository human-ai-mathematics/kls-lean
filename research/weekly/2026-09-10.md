# KLS formalization — weekly update

**Reporting period:** 4–10 September 2026

**Prepared for:** Nicolas

**Project:** kls-lean

## Overall progress

This week I completed the Lean formalization of the equivalence between the isotropic, covariance-normalized, and affine KLS formulations, together with the analytic foundations needed for that reduction. The library now connects its relaxed-energy formulation to the literature Poincaré constant for every finite-dimensional log-concave probability law, including laws supported on a proper affine subspace. The dimension-free KLS bound itself remains open.

With much of the shared infrastructure now in place, I expect to move faster on the next formalization tasks. The focus can increasingly shift from building the foundations to using them in further proofs.

## Completed this week

- **Affine-to-isotropic reduction.** For each fixed constant K, I proved the pairwise equivalence of the three formulations. The proof includes centering, covariance whitening, and transport of the relaxed energy. This makes the reduction to isotropic laws available as a checked theorem for subsequent work.
- **Log-concave analytic foundations.** I formalized the Borell density and moment representation on the intrinsic affine support, weighted gradient approximation, and the smooth/locally Lipschitz Poincaré constant comparisons. These results discharge the intrinsic absolute-continuity premise from log-concavity and cover singular ambient laws and zero-dimensional support.
- **Closed covariance energy.** I proved dense domain, closability, core independence, and the identification of the covariance-weighted relaxation with a closed quadratic form. I also proved translation invariance of the affine Poincaré constant. These supply the analytic starting point for later operator and spectral arguments.
- **Benchmark and library consolidation.** The sharp one-dimensional Gaussian Poincaré theorem was completed this week. I also reorganized the public APIs and module layout around the mathematical objects, with checks that the refactor preserved statement and definition content. Previously completed finite tensorization remains available as supporting infrastructure.

## What remains open

There are two separate kinds of remaining work. On the formalization side, the concrete normal-contraction estimate and Dirichlet property, identification with Cheeger energy under the normalization 2 Ch, and the required generator/spectral interfaces are still unproved. Equality of optimal Poincaré constants does not by itself establish equality of energies.

On the research side, the candidate routes to a dimension-free bound still retain open assumptions. The completed reductions do not settle those assumptions, and finite tensorization alone does not complete the product case for arbitrary one-dimensional log-concave factors.

## Plan for next week

- **Normal contraction and the Dirichlet property.** My first target is the concrete normal-contraction estimate for the selected relaxed energy. I plan to make the approximation and contraction arguments explicit, prove the estimate, and use it to construct the Dirichlet witness. This would extend the existing closed quadratic form with the additional structure needed for subsequent analytic arguments.
- **Cheeger and spectral interfaces.** Building on that work, I plan to develop the next bridge toward Cheeger–Poincaré comparison and clarify the prerequisites for a self-adjoint generator and spectral-gap formulation. I will separate the interfaces that can already be implemented from those that depend on further analytic results, and begin with the available pieces.
- **A concrete route toward KLS.** I plan to review the candidate routes against the completed affine/isotropic reduction and identify a suitable next bridge to formalize. The aim is to turn the broader roadmap into a sequence of specific proof tasks, with the remaining research assumptions stated explicitly. Route selection is a point I would like to discuss with you.
- **Proof architecture and usability.** The repository is currently private, and we plan to open-source it later. In preparation, I will continue to devote substantial time to making the library easier to understand, use, and contribute to. This includes organizing proofs around reusable intermediate lemmas, improving API consistency and readability, and adding focused examples and documentation for the main interfaces. These improvements should make subsequent proofs easier to develop and help others review and build on the formalization.

The normal-contraction theorem and Dirichlet witness are the immediate proof targets; the Cheeger, spectral, and route-specific work will proceed as their prerequisites become available. With the shared foundations largely established, I expect a faster pace of proof development while continuing to improve the structure and readability of the library.
