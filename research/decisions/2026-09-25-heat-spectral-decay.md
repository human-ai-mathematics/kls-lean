## Scope and placement

The actual heat operator is generated by the closed weighted gradient of a
full-dimensional log-concave probability law. The new property module is
`HeatSemigroup/Decay.lean`. It imports the existing Markov, domain and generated
time-derivative interfaces. The probability-law identity for a centered L2
indicator belongs in `L2/MeanVariance.lean`. The public aggregator imports the
new property module. Existing signatures are unchanged.

The only spectral assumption is explicit: `C > 0` and, for every `u` in the
form domain of the actual closed gradient `T`, `‖center u‖² ≤ C ‖T u‖²`.
The statement does not assume heat decay or an optimal constant.

## Proof boundary

- Markov symmetry and the fixed constant one preserve the mean. Linearity fixes
  all constants, so centering commutes with heat evolution.
- For arbitrary L2 input and every positive time, the existing smoothing theorem
  puts the actual heat orbit in `D(A)` for `A=T* T`. The strong derivative is
  `-A P_t f`; the derivative of its squared L2 norm is exactly
  `-2 ‖T P_t f‖²`.
- Apply the form-domain gap to the centered orbit. The derivative of
  `exp(2t/C) ‖P_t(center f)‖²` is nonpositive on positive time. Strong
  continuity at zero gives the bound for every `t ≥ 0` and every `f ∈ L²`.
- The half-time semigroup law and self-adjointness identify
  `⟪center f, P_t(center f)⟫ = ‖P_(t/2)(center f)‖²`. Consequently the
  correlation exponent is `-t/C`; the squared-norm exponent is `-2t/C`.
- The generic L2 indicator identity gives
  `‖center 1_D‖² = ν(D)(1-ν(D))` for every measurable `D`, including masses
  zero and one. The heat statements include time zero.
