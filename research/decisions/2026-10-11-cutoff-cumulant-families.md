`Analysis/Cutoff/{Profile,Integrability,Conservativity}.lean` owns generic cutoff functions and the analytic conclusions obtained from their estimates. Profile contains real-variable smooth cutoff, majorant, primitive, rescaling and exhaustion tools. Integrability contains the Fatou argument on an arbitrary measurable space with a measure. Conservativity contains the probability-measure Fatou/dominated-convergence argument with a bounded signed function and vanishing cutoff defect. These share an object but not an identical dependency level. Keep separate files and their exact imports: in particular, do not merge the latter two or import Integrability from Conservativity. Extra imports can alter instance terms even without a textual statement change. Geometric cutoffs, inverse-Hessian operators and concrete integration-by-parts clients remain outside this generic family.

`Analysis/BakryEmery/Cumulant/{Basic,Local,Leibniz}.lean` owns the logarithmic Laplace transform and its cumulant tensors. Basic is foundational because it defines `logLaplace` and `cumulant` and supplies their prerequisite identities; the existing estimates in that file are retained. Local supplies the one-positive-exponential-radius results, second and third moments, covariance identification, compact-support consequences, cumulant Riesz vectors and gradient jets. Leibniz supplies deterministic spatial expansions of the quadratic form of the centered CGF gradient. Retain the existing Local-to-Basic and Leibniz-to-Local dependency direction and all other imports. The established BakryEmery location remains appropriate at the tilt/Taylor-tensor boundary; this change does not introduce a new abstract probability hierarchy.

The one-radius and all-radii hypotheses remain distinct. `LetwinQuadraticBound` remains an explicit conditional premise. The raw Leibniz formula retains arbitrary continuous linear M, and the grouped covariance-inverse formula retains its actual hypotheses. Log-concave adapters and BKL-specific constructions remain under KLS/BKL; generic modules acquire no per-paper imports. No stochastic differential, unconditional Letwin estimate, or additional paper theorem is obtained by changing directories.
