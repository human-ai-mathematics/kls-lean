# Bakry--Emery Route: Generator Identification, Bochner Interface and Buser Exit

Date: 2026-09-20.

This record is a route review, not a Buser proof. Currently repository has been constructed from the closed weighted gradient
`T` to yield `A = T*T >= 0` and `P_t = exp(-tA)`, and the said `L2` Markov property has been proven; this round of audit document
`research/audits/BakryEmeryRouteStatements.lean` only precisely type-checks the next batch of propositions. It does not introduce
`axiom`, `sorry`, or placeholder proofs, nor does it embed the full Buser goal into the assumptions.

The endpoint is preserved: for any finite-dimensional, arbitrary (degenerate, possibly with boundary, possibly non-smooth) log-concave probability law `mu`, prove

```text
Psi_KLS(mu)^2 <= pi * C_P^lit(mu).
```

A smooth full-dimensional model is merely an intermediate layer for establishing an analysis engine and cannot replace this endpoint.

## 3. Smooth convex potential model

Fix a finite-dimensional real inner product space `E`, Haar/Lebesgue measure `m`, and potential function `U : E -> R`. Requirements:

1. `U` at least `C-infinity` (local Bochner itself only uses finite order, but semigroup regularity should initially retain infinite smoothness);
2. `ConvexOn R univ U`;
3. `exp(-U)` is integrable over `m`. Convexity alone does not imply this;
4. The normalization constant `Z = int exp(-U) dm` satisfies `0 < Z < infinity`, and takes
   `mu = Z^{-1} exp(-U) m`, which in Mathlib can be expressed via `m.tilted (-U)`;
5. The initial test function is taken as `C_c^infinity(E)`, so the function, its gradient, and finite-order derivatives belong to the required
   `Lp` under `mu`;
6. The interior point of the support of the full-dimensional model is non-empty. For a smooth potential that is finite everywhere, the goal is to further prove that the support is `univ`, rather than taking this fact as a default in the construction field.

Write

```text
L f = Delta f - <grad U, grad f>,
T f = grad f,
A = T*T,
P_t = exp(-t A).
```

The symbol target is `A f = -L f`, so the differential equation of the existing semigroup is `d/dt P_t f = L P_t f`.

## 5. Gamma, Gamma2 and Bochner

Using the diffusion generator `L`:

```text
Gamma(f,g) = (L(fg) - f Lg - g Lf)/2,
Gamma(f,g) = <grad f, grad g>,
Gamma2(f) = L(Gamma(f,f))/2 - Gamma(f,Lf).
```

### APIs that can be directly used

  `laplacian_eq_iteratedFDeriv_orthonormalBasis`;
- `bilinearIteratedFDerivTwo`;
- `stdOrthonormalBasis`;

  `Analysis.BakryEmery.bilinearIteratedFDerivTwo_symm`, which has been encapsulated as the Hessian symmetry theorem (B2) for direct use in the path.

- No suitable named Hilbert--Schmidt norm and basis-independence theorem for the current Hessian representation;
- No existing Laplacian product formula, `grad ||grad f||^2`, `Delta ||grad f||^2`, and the full suite of rewrite rules for Hessian contraction;
- Still needs to connect `C2` convexity to `bilinearIteratedFDerivTwo R U x v v >= 0`.

Thus the first version of the Bochner proposition should honestly fix `stdOrthonormalBasis`:

```text
Gamma2(f)(x)
  = sum_i sum_j (D2 f(x)(e_i,e_j))^2
    + D2 U(x)(grad f(x), grad f(x)).
```

Only after proving the basis independence of the double sum can the public be derived.
`||Hess f||_HS^2 + Hess U(grad f,grad f)` Notation. By the non-negativity of each square term and the semi-positiveness of the Hessian of the potential we obtain
`Gamma2(f) >= 0`. The audit documents B0--B4 precisely record these interfaces; B2 is proven, B0, B1, B3,

## 6. From local Bochner connection to existing `P_t`

The Bochner identity itself does not produce the exchange of semigroup gradients. The following bridge is still missing.

1. **Representatives and regularity.** `P_t[f]` is currently just an equivalence class of `L2`. To apply classical `gradient`, one must prove
   At `t>0`, there exist sufficiently smooth representatives, or alternatively, use a closed gradient `T(P_t f)`.
2. **Domain stability.** Requires `P_t` to preserve `D(T)`, and to maintain a sufficiently high generator domain in the squared Gamma derivation;
   Test functions are not necessarily preserved by `P_t` as having compact support.
3. **Time derivative.** For typical interpolation quantity
   `s -> P_s Gamma(P_{t-s}f)` Prove differentiability, and prove that the derivative equals `2 P_s Gamma2(P_{t-s}f)`.
4. **Exchange and limits.** Requires legitimate exchange `P_s`, space derivatives, integrals, and time limits, and closes the test class closure to
   Sobolev/BV class.
5. **Square estimate.** First from `Gamma2 >= 0` get
   `Gamma(P_t f) <= P_t Gamma(f)`.
6. **Self-improving.** The sharp route still requires the `alpha in [1/2,1]` from the literature (27), particularly
   `|T P_t f| <= P_t |T f|`. One cannot automatically treat step 5 as step 6.
7. **Reverse estimation of BGL.** Still need to prove Proposition 3.1/(32), then derive (33) from it.
   `L-infinity -> gradient L-infinity` Heat regularization.

Only after these interfaces are processed do we enter the unlisted small-time BV estimates for this round of acceptance, then assemble with the final Buser.

## 7. Energy and time normalization

Under the current closed gradient convention:

```text
||T f||_2^2 = E^can(f) = SmoothGradient.relaxation(f) = 2 Ch(f),
A = T*T,
P_t = exp(-t A),
d/dt ||P_t f||_2^2 = -2 ||T P_t f||_2^2.
```

The last three terms are compatible with the Rayleigh numerator convention of De Ponti--Mondino `int |Df|^2`; if one mistakenly treats `Ch` itself as a quadratic form of an operator, a time factor of 2 would arise, causing the final constant to drift.

This round adds `KLS/LogConcave/CanonicalEnergy.lean`, proving the previously missing named bridge:

- `weightedRelaxation_id_eq_smoothGradient` Perform pairwise comparison between two test domains and their energies;

  canonical energy;
- `closedWeightedOperator_id_energy_eq_two_mul_cheeger` Ultimately yield `||Tf||^2 = 2 Ch(f)`.

Thus the audit document E0 has transitioned from a candidate interface to a proved statement. The proof does not rely on naming to establish equality of two energies: in one direction, a smooth-core recovery derived from Borell density is used, and in the other direction, a global Lipschitz operator test is embedded into the local-slope test class, followed by the use of the previously certified smooth/local-slope to identify it.

Spectral gap norm is defined as

```text
Var_mu(f) <= C_P^lit(mu) * ||T f||_2^2,
lambda_1 = 1 / C_P^lit(mu).
```

Thus expected `||P_t f||_2 <= exp(-lambda_1 t) ||f||_2` (mean zero), the exponential of the squared norm is

## 8. From smooth full-dimensional models to arbitrary log-concave laws

The recommended main export is a **convex potential/closed form approximation on intrinsic affine supports**, since repository already has `intrinsicMeasure`, intrinsic Haar measure, closed weighted gradients, and canonical energy. Directly declaring the current object as an RCD space would introduce larger unformalized black boxes; pre-interpreting the support boundary as classical Neumann boundary lacks justification.

### 8.1 Dimension reduction and Dirac

- First move any `mu` to the direction space of its affine support; here the intrinsic law is full-dimensional.
- In positive dimension, work in this space, then transfer the Poincare constant, isoperimetric scale, energy, and semigroup conclusions back.
- For zero-dimensional/Dirac case, prove separately: `Psi_KLS = 0`, `C_P^lit = 0`, without hard-coding through the heat flow limit.

### 8.2 Non-smooth potentials and boundaries

A general full-dimensional log-concave law can be written as `exp(-W) dx`, where `W` is a lower-semicontinuous convex function taking extended-real values;
The domain of `W = +infinity` is exactly the support constraint. Candidate approximation:

1. Obtain a finite, convex, `C1` potential via the Moreau--Yosida envelope; the Moreau envelope itself is not `C-infinity`;
2. Convolve to smooth, and add `epsilon ||x||^2` to ensure integrability and strict tail behavior;
3. Re-normalize, and if necessary, apply a constant shift to the approximating potential;
4. Let both the smoothing scale and `epsilon` tend to zero simultaneously, so that the probability law weakly converges to the target law, including cases with convex support boundaries.

The boundary conditions should arise from the limit of closed forms; they may manifest as natural/reflection boundaries, but one cannot first assume a classical Neumann trace theory and then claim equivalence to the current closed gradient.

### 8.3 Must-proven stability

- Provide a unified transport/embedding framework on the `L2(mu_n)` of varying measures;
- Mosco convergence of closed quadratic forms;
- Strong convergence of resolvent `(I+A_n)^{-1}` and semigroup `P_t^n`;
- Stability of `alpha=1/2` gradient estimates and BGL estimates in the limit;
- lower semicontinuous of BV total variation/Perimeter and recovery sequence, thus transferring small-time estimates to the target law;
- Directional lower semicontinuity of Poincare constants, Cheeger infimum, and final `pi` constants;
- Above convergence and constant preservation during affine dimension reduction.

If instead we follow the RCD route, we still need to formalize: the weighted Euclidean distance measure on closed convex support bands is `RCD(0,infinity)`, the current Cheeger energy/semigroup is consistent with the RCD object, and De Ponti--Mondino's BV perimeter is consistent with repository's Minkowski/relative-perimeter infimum. This approach directly references the main theorem mathematically, but is further away from the current Lean API; thus, it is not recommended as the first implementation route.
